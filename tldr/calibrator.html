What's plotted

Each line is the worst-case position error when you reconstruct the orbit with node points spaced h seconds apart. Find your budget on the vertical axis, slide right to a line, drop down — that spacing is the card's answer. Below ~90 s the four orbits spread out by five orders of magnitude: the same spacing that's overkill for a navigation satellite is far too coarse for a fast, stretched Molniya orbit.

The two velocities

The propagator reports a velocity at each point, but it isn't exactly the slope of the reported positions. Feeding that raw velocity into the curve adds an error that grows with spacing. Recomputing the slope from the positions (a differentiation step) removes it:

error(h) ≈ curvature·h⁴/384 + velocity-gap·h

Where the second term dominates (navigation & geostationary orbits), differentiating lets you space nodes much farther apart for the same budget. Where the first term already rules (low orbit), it barely helps — only closer nodes do.
